A Decade of Flight (2016-2026)

Tracking the global orbital launch success rate over the last ten years reveals a distinct upward trend, despite the exponential increase in total launch volume. The slight dip around 2021-2022 correlates with the mass introduction of untested small-lift commercial vehicles.

Failure Diagnostics & Root Causes

When launches fail, the causes are complex. This section breaks down the 42 recorded failures over the past decade. Interact with the chart or the buttons below to explore the specific technical areas responsible for mission loss.

Propulsion Systems (45%)

The most common cause of mission failure. Issues typically arise in the first stage turbo-pumps, combustion instability, or premature engine shutdown. The extreme thermal and acoustic environments make liquid propulsion the highest-risk element of any ascent trajectory.

Performance by Vehicle Class

Not all rockets are created equal. Compare the success rates and total launch volumes across Heavy, Medium, and Small lift vehicles. Use the toggle to filter between the entire decade and recent data.

Heavy Lift (>20,000kg)

Dominated by established players and highly mature reusable platforms. Lowest failure rate due to intense QA and legacy engineering.

Medium Lift (2,000 - 20,000kg)

The workhorse of global telecommunications and government observation. Highly stable, with failures mostly limited to expendable legacy systems.

SmallSat Lift (<2,000kg)

High volume, higher risk. Many new startup entrants driving the failure rate up, though rapid iteration means rapid improvement.
